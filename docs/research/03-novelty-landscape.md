# Novelty landscape and prior-art comparison

**Search date:** 2026-08-07
**Method:** targeted online search of scholarly indexes, publisher pages, primary project pages, and systems named by relevant papers.

## Important scope note

This is a **design-research novelty review**, not:

- a systematic literature review;
- a patentability or freedom-to-operate opinion;
- a complete commercial-product survey;
- proof that no earlier system contains a similar feature.

The review is suitable for refining research claims and selecting experiments. Any formal “first system” claim needs a registered systematic search protocol and, if commercially relevant, professional patent analysis.

## Closest systems and research

### Critsly — closest design-education system

[Critsly: An Artefact-Aware AI Critique Teammate for Design Education and Project-Based Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09673) presents a visual board containing design intentions, board elements, annotations, links, and critique history. It combines structured reflection, multi-perspective critique, action planning, optional peer/jury settings, and educator evidence traces.

**Overlap with Critique HUD research**

- AI as critique teammate rather than generic chatbot;
- design intent;
- structured reflection;
- multi-perspective critique;
- action planning;
- peer/jury workflows;
- evidence for educators.

**Remaining differentiation opportunity**

- co-present spoken critique captured live from a shared microphone;
- speaker-attributed public/private cognitive mirror;
- restrained in-the-moment facilitation with source-linked guards;
- explicit typed lineage from spoken feedback to later artifact revision;
- acoustic simulation and fault-injection validation of the live path.

Critsly makes it unsafe to claim that artifact-aware AI critique, intent grounding, personas, action plans, or peer/jury modes are individually novel.

### MeetMap — closest real-time mapping system

[MeetMap: Real-Time Collaborative Dialogue Mapping with LLMs in Online Meetings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01564) transcribes speaker turns and creates real-time summary nodes and dialogue maps. It compares a human-built map from AI nodes with an AI-built, human-editable map.

**Overlap**

- speaker recognition and live transcription;
- real-time cognitive scaffolding;
- non-linear discussion representation;
- editable AI-generated map;
- cognitive-load motivation.

**Differentiation opportunity**

- architecture-studio critique rather than general online meetings;
- an editable student intent contract;
- public/private role separation;
- protections against person-level judgments;
- preservation of minority positions;
- feedback-to-revision provenance;
- physical-room acoustic workflow.

MeetMap means “LLM-generated real-time discussion map” is not a novel claim.

### MeetScript — closest transcript-interaction system

[MeetScript: Designing Transcript-based Interactions to Support Active Participation in Group Video Meetings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12115) provides live transcript bubbles that participants can edit, tag, comment on, highlight, and like. Some interactions are anonymous, comments may be private, and low-value transcript content fades to reduce cognitive load. Its study included 80 users in 22 teams.

**Overlap**

- live transcript as parallel participation surface;
- correction and annotation;
- public/private contributions;
- anonymous interactions;
- cognitive-load reduction.

**Differentiation opportunity**

- design-intent alignment;
- critique-specific discourse relations;
- facilitator/public asymmetry;
- longitudinal connection to artifact revisions;
- architecture-specific psychosocial rules.

MeetScript means transcript interaction, anonymous annotations, and private contextual feedback are established prior art.

### Design Critique AI (DCAI) — closest instructor-reviewed critique generator

[Design Critique Reimagined: Instructors’ Expectations of AI Feedback](https://dl.designresearchsociety.org/iasdr/iasdr2025/fullpapers/113/) evaluates multimodal-LLM critiques with ten industrial-design instructors and 678 coded responses. Instructors requested contextual sensitivity, deeper reasoning, design-intent alignment, pedagogical flexibility, and emotional support.

**Overlap**

- multimodal design critique;
- structured and accessible novice feedback;
- intent alignment;
- emotional support;
- human–AI collaboration.

**Differentiation opportunity**

- the critique ecology rather than the quality of generated critique;
- student-authored and versioned intent;
- live human-human discussion mediation;
- provenance and learner disposition;
- no automated evaluator role.

DCAI weakens claims that intent-aligned, emotionally supportive, structured AI design feedback is new.

### ArchiJury — closest architectural visual critic

[ArchiJury: Exploring the Capabilities of Vision-Language Models to Generate Architectural Critique](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/jcode/article/1618548) uses a domain-specific dataset curated by architects. Its first phase classifies visual attributes and its second generates critique guided by context, form, and program criteria.

**Overlap**

- architecture-specific critique;
- visual evidence;
- context and predefined criteria.

**Differentiation opportunity**

- mediation of human critique rather than automated architectural evaluation;
- student intent and agency;
- social and power dynamics;
- live dialogue and revision lineage.

ArchiJury makes “AI-generated architectural critique from visual artifacts” an occupied claim.

### Reflection in co-action — closest theoretical framing

[Reflection in co-action: AI joins the crit](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10798-026-10079-6) synthesizes 16 studies from 134 screened records and frames architectural learning as “learning by co-doing.” It identifies phase-dependent integration, new studio literacies, pedagogical scaffolds, and governance tensions.

**Overlap**

- reflection-in-action;
- AI as part of the critique and learning process;
- agency and accountability;
- phase dependence.

**Differentiation opportunity**

- a concrete information architecture and interaction policy that operationalize co-doing;
- measurable provenance and intervention restraint;
- deployment and evaluation in co-present studio critique.

The broad theoretical reframing of AI as a reflective partner in architectural studio is no longer unique by itself.

### CoDialogue Space — closest real-time AI facilitator

[CoDialogue Space: Design of AI-supported Collaborative Learning Platform](https://2026.isls.org/docs/General%20Volume%202026.pdf) describes a multiuser collaborative-learning environment with real-time generative-AI facilitation, adaptive facilitation levels, instructor-configurable prompts, and goal-oriented tasks.

**Overlap**

- real-time group dialogue;
- AI facilitation;
- instructor control;
- learning-goal context.

**Differentiation opportunity**

- passive co-present audio capture rather than platform-entered conversation;
- architecture-specific critique relations and student intent;
- public/private cognitive mirror;
- evidence-linked correction and revision lineage;
- intervention guards against person-level inference.

CoDialogue Space means real-time multiuser AI facilitation is established prior work.

### CADA and real-time classroom dashboards — closest learning analytics

[CADA](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8982662/) combines structural and content analysis of online discussions in a teacher-facing learning-analytics dashboard developed through participatory design. The broader dashboard literature already includes real-time participation, progress, misconception alerts, and teacher orchestration.

[Speaker Diarization in the Classroom](https://www.educationaldatamining.org/edm2024/proceedings/2024.EDM-short-papers.33/) investigates talk-time measurement in real group discussions using diarization.

**Overlap**

- participation analytics;
- discourse patterns;
- instructor awareness;
- speaker/talk-time analysis.

**Differentiation opportunity**

- learner-facing cognitive mirror rather than instructor surveillance;
- no competence or personality scoring;
- intent, source evidence, and artifact revision;
- two-surface governance;
- critique-specific actionability.

Participation bars, diarization, and teacher analytics should not be presented as novel.

### EFFORT-AI — closest agency-preserving learning model

[EFFORT-AI: an effort-preserving module for cognitively aligned human-AI learning](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1849821/full) argues for separate diagnostic, first-attempt, constrained-feedback, schema-organization, metacognitive-mirror, and transfer modes. It treats delayed answers, learner artifacts, adaptive hints, and fading as core safeguards.

**Overlap**

- separate interaction modes;
- metacognitive mirror;
- constrained help;
- preserving effort and human judgment;
- process traces.

**Differentiation opportunity**

- application to social, co-present design critique;
- explicit identity/audience control;
- human-human feedback mediation;
- speech-to-revision provenance.

“Preserving learner effort through constrained AI modes” is a shared principle, not a unique contribution.

### Additional adjacent systems

- [Socratique](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02962) combines gamification and generative-AI assistance to improve peer-review clarity, relevance, and specificity in software-engineering education.
- [SPHERE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16513) uses structured human review of LLM output for scalable personalized programming feedback.
- [An Explanation-Based Classroom Response System](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/42114) aggregates LLM analysis of student explanations to support real-time instructor pivots.
- [Adobe Agentic Design Review System](https://research.adobe.com/news/agentic-design-review-system-teaching-ai-to-review-graphic-designs-the-way-experts-do/) uses specialized agents and a design-review benchmark for actionable graphic-design feedback.

These systems further constrain claims around AI-assisted peer feedback, human review of AI feedback, real-time classroom analysis, and specialized design critique.

## Iterative novelty tests

### Test 1 — “AI cognitive mirror for architecture critique”

**Result: weak as a standalone novelty claim.**

The local papers, Critsly, Reflection in co-action, DCAI, and EFFORT-AI all occupy adjacent versions of reflective or collaborative AI. Use this as framing, not the sole contribution.

### Test 2 — “Real-time LLM discussion map”

**Result: not novel.**

MeetMap directly implements and evaluates it. Critique HUD needs domain-specific relations, intent alignment, governance, and revision provenance to differentiate.

### Test 3 — “Anonymous, safer peer critique”

**Result: not novel alone.**

Anonymous peer review predates LLMs, MeetScript supports anonymous transcript interactions, and Critsly includes anonymous/reveal modes. Novelty must come from how identity policy interacts with spoken critique, evidence, learner control, and accountability.

### Test 4 — “Multimodal architectural AI critic”

**Result: not novel and misaligned with the research if framed as evaluator.**

ArchiJury and DCAI occupy visual design critique. The stronger alternative is a multimodal **reflective twin** that compares declared intent, artifact evidence, and human critique without issuing a final judgment.

### Test 5 — “Real-time AI facilitator in education”

**Result: not novel alone.**

CoDialogue Space and classroom-response systems already provide real-time AI-supported facilitation or instructional pivots.

### Test 6 — “Student-controlled critique provenance”

**Result: promising and comparatively differentiated.**

The reviewed systems contain pieces—intent, evidence traces, dialogue maps, action plans, annotations—but the explicit chain below was not found as a central architecture:

```text
versioned student intent
  → speaker-attributed source phrase
  → typed critique relation
  → student interpretation/correction
  → accept/adapt/defer/reject disposition
  → owned action
  → artifact-region revision
  → subsequent reflection and re-critique
```

This should be the primary novelty hypothesis.

### Test 7 — “Agency budget for public AI intervention”

**Result: promising as a design mechanism, not yet validated.**

The current specification combines:

- one prompt at a time;
- no person-level labels;
- no public confidence values;
- visible source evidence;
- editable intent;
- human corrections;
- silence when confidence/evidence is insufficient.

Related work supports constrained help, but this explicit public-intervention contract for co-present critique appears comparatively distinctive. It needs formalization and study.

### Test 8 — “Acoustic digital twin for critique-system evaluation”

**Result: technically promising.**

Generating grounded multi-speaker critique scripts, rendering distinct voices with controlled overlap, playing them acoustically through the same room/microphone path, and scoring transcription, discourse mapping, guard behavior, and HUD latency is a valuable systems-research contribution. The current code implements much of generation/mixing but not the complete sim B path or valid end-to-end metrics.

## Defensible novelty statement

Recommended:

> We investigate an agency-preserving information system for co-present architecture critiques that turns live, speaker-attributed dialogue into a correctable public/private critique provenance graph grounded in the student’s versioned design intent and later artifact revisions. Unlike automated design critics or general meeting maps, the system constrains public AI intervention, preserves dissent, and makes the learner’s response to feedback—not the AI’s verdict—the durable unit of record.

Avoid:

- “the first AI architecture critic”;
- “the first real-time LLM discussion map”;
- “the first anonymous AI peer-review platform”;
- “objective or unbiased critique”;
- “AI detects bias” without a narrow, validated operational definition;
- “improves learning” before a comparative deployment;
- “understands architecture” based only on text classification.

## Research gap that remains after comparison

The strongest gap is the absence of a complete, evaluated system that joins all of the following:

1. real co-present critique speech;
2. architecture-specific intent and artifact context;
3. a source-traceable map of critique relations;
4. student-controlled interpretation and disposition;
5. privacy/identity governance across public and private surfaces;
6. feedback-to-revision continuity;
7. restrained, evidence-gated facilitation;
8. evaluation against both learning outcomes and agency harms.

That integrated gap is narrower than the original “AI as reflection partner” framing, but it is more defensible and more useful.
